# Case 03 — Live evidence for waiting during proposal generation

Product context below is hypothetical. External research must use real, verifiable sources.

## Task

We are designing PitchDraft, a tool that generates a client proposal from a consultant's outline. Consultants may want to keep working while it generates. We have no interviews or customer analytics yet. The engineer estimates generation may take 20 seconds to 4 minutes; this is an estimate, not a measured latency distribution. The service exposes queued, generating, ready, and failed states, but no reliable percentage complete. Draft jobs continue if the user leaves the page.

Our decision is how to communicate waiting and let someone leave safely. Candidate directions are a percentage bar, an estimated time range, or named stages with a way to return to the draft. Explore alternatives if the evidence suggests them.

Web access is available for this case. Research on the actual execution date and record that date. Find a small set of relevant original studies and/or authoritative first-party design guidance. Examine support and limitations for these directions, and explain how well the research transfers to this task and uncertain duration. Propose the smallest useful prototype comparison for a team with three design days.

We need a concise decision brief with links to the source pages you actually inspected, the dates and methods you can verify, uncertainties, and a minimal search log. Do not treat the hypothetical product context as observed customer behavior. If a source is inaccessible, make the access limit visible and continue with what you can verify.
